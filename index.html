KNOW THE SIGNS

Common red flags to watch for

One warning sign does not prove a message is a scam. Several together mean you should slow down and verify.

!

Urgency and threats

“Act in 10 minutes or your account will close.” Scammers pressure you to act before thinking.

Pressure tactic
₦

Too-good-to-be-true offers

Guaranteed profits, surprise prizes, or a job that asks you to pay upfront deserve extra scrutiny.

Unrealistic promise
@

Odd sender or link

Look for misspellings, unexpected domains, shortened links, or addresses that imitate a real organization.

Check the source
🔒

Requests for secrets

Never share one-time passcodes, passwords, PINs, or recovery phrases with someone who contacts you.

Sensitive information
↗

Unusual payment methods

Be cautious when asked to pay with gift cards, crypto, or transfers to an unrelated personal account.

Hard to reverse
👤

Impersonation

A message may pretend to come from a bank, employer, delivery company, friend, or government agency.

Verify independently

LEARN BY EXAMPLE

Messages that deserve a closer look

These are fictional examples for education. Real scams can look polished, and legitimate messages can sometimes be awkwardly written.

!
“Bank Security”Example SMS · Fictional
High risk

Your account is locked. Verify immediately at secure-bank-example.invalid or access will be removed.

Why it is suspicious
  • Threatens account loss to create panic.
  • Uses an unfamiliar domain.
  • Asks you to follow a message link.
₦
“Recruitment Team”Example job offer · Fictional
High risk

Congratulations! You have been selected for a remote job. Pay a refundable registration fee today to secure your position.

Why it is suspicious
  • Requests money before work begins.
  • Promises a job without a credible process.
  • Uses “refundable” to make payment feel safe.
★
“Prize Desk”Example social DM · Fictional
Check first

You won a phone in our giveaway! Send your one-time code and delivery fee to claim your prize.

Why it is suspicious
  • Unexpected prize you did not enter for.
  • Requests a one-time security code.
  • Demands a fee to release the reward.
💡

Safe habit: Do not use the link or phone number in a suspicious message. Open the official app, type the known website address yourself, or contact the organization using a trusted number.

ACT QUICKLY, WITHOUT PANIC

If you think you have been scammed

Acting promptly can limit further loss. The right steps depend on what information or money was exposed.

01

Stop contact and payments

Do not send more money, pay “recovery” fees, or follow further instructions from the suspected scammer.

02

Contact your bank or payment provider

Use the official app or number. Explain what happened, ask whether a transfer can be stopped or disputed, and secure affected cards or accounts.

03

Secure your accounts

Change compromised passwords from a trusted device, enable multi-factor authentication, sign out other sessions, and contact the provider if you shared a code.

04

Save evidence

Keep messages, usernames, receipts, transaction references, phone numbers, email headers, and website addresses. Do not alter originals if you may report the case.

05

Report it and warn others safely

Report the account or message to the platform and contact the appropriate local law-enforcement or cybercrime reporting service. Tell trusted people without publishing sensitive personal details.

ⓘ

In Nigeria? Contact your bank promptly through its official channel. You can also seek guidance from the Nigeria Police Force National Cybercrime Centre and the appropriate consumer-protection or financial regulator. Use official government websites to find current reporting channels. Never pay someone who guarantees they can recover your money.
